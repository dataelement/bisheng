# Swiss Signal Grid · Design System

This original 16:9 system turns management evidence into a precise Swiss information field. Paper white, pure black, signal red, a strict modular grid, oversized numerals, vertical indexing, and chart-led compositions create a disciplined analytical voice. Every visible item in the reference deck remains an editable PPTD object.

## Communication model

Use this system for executive reviews, market intelligence, operating-model comparisons, risk concentration, and market-entry decisions. Each page starts with one management conclusion, lets the evidence occupy the main grid, and ends with a concrete implication, threshold, or action.

1. State the decision or finding in the title before presenting supporting evidence.
2. Give one dataset or comparison primary visual control of each page.
3. Use signal red only for the selected metric, risk, threshold, or action.
4. Keep source dates, units, statistical updates, and derived calculations next to the relevant evidence.
5. Preserve a consistent vertical index and page coordinate so the sequence reads as one system.

## Visual signature

The deck behaves like a printed information system rather than a dashboard. A black index rail fixes the left boundary. Thin rules expose the column logic. Oversized numbers become compositional anchors. Signal red cuts across the grid only when the audience must notice a material change. Square geometry, hard edges, asymmetric scale, and generous negative space keep the pages exact without becoming empty.

### Color palette

- Paper background `#F5F3EE`
- White surface `#FFFFFF`
- Pure black text and structure `#111111`
- Warm gray secondary text `#6E6D68`
- Hairline grid `#CBC8C0`
- Pale structural wash `#E8E5DE`
- Signal red `#E52333`

Paper occupies 65–85% of normal analytical pages. Black establishes typography, axes, table headers, and the index rail. Red stays below 15% on most pages and identifies one focal message. Full black pages are reserved for capability shifts and final decisions.

### Typography

- Chinese titles, body, and numerals: `HarmonyOS Sans SC`, falling back to the host CJK sans serif.
- Latin section labels use the same family in uppercase with increased tracking.
- Page titles: bold, left aligned, 27–34 px on the 960 × 540 PPTD canvas.
- Hero metrics: bold, 38–68 px, paired with a compact basis line.
- Body and annotations: 11–15 px with short lines and explicit alignment to the grid.
- Sources and calculation notes: 7–9 px, muted, and kept outside the primary visual field.

### Grid and furniture

- Canvas: PPTD v2, `960 × 540`, 16:9.
- Use a strict 12-column mental grid with a narrow left index column and an 18–24 px baseline rhythm.
- Main content begins on the same vertical axis after the index rail.
- Titles, chart edges, table columns, and conclusion bands share grid lines.
- Use thin black or gray rules to expose relationships. Rounded cards, floating shadows, and decorative icon rows do not belong to this system.
- Crop English section labels or vertical numerals deliberately at page edges to create continuity without reducing Chinese readability.

## Reusable components

1. **Vertical index rail**: a black or red strip carrying the section code, page number, and sequence marker.
2. **Signal metric**: one oversized numeral with a short denominator, period, and calculation basis.
3. **Evidence spine**: a chart or table aligned across several columns and used as the page's structural backbone.
4. **Decision band**: a black or red horizontal block containing the management implication.
5. **Split field**: two unequal evidence regions separated by a rule for comparison, concentration, or mode choice.
6. **Quadrant gate**: four hard-edged zones that compare potential with operational control.
7. **Dark transition page**: a black field with numbered capability lines and one red shift statement.

## Charts and tables

- Charts participate in the page grid; they are never dropped into a decorative card.
- Use black for the base structure, signal red for the current or selected series, and warm gray for context.
- Prefer direct labels, compact axes, hairline guides, and square bars.
- Preserve decimals with editable text labels when native Office chart formatting changes the disclosed value.
- Comparison tables use a black header, paper body, thin gray borders, and one red implication line.
- Every table, chart, or matrix closes with a management reading rather than a neutral caption.

## Page rhythm

The reference sequence covers a thesis cover, three-signal summary, scale trajectory, export structure, concentration evidence, warehouse operating chain, mode comparison, capability shift, market-entry gate, and two audience-specific action paths. Reuse these page roles as a vocabulary while adapting their order to the next presentation.

Alternate paper analytical pages with one or two black transition or decision pages. Vary the dominant structure across large numerals, charts, split comparisons, matrices, process strips, and action lists. Avoid repeating an equal-card composition.

## Prohibited treatments

- Empty minimalism with large unused areas and weak evidence density.
- Repeated same-size rounded cards, generic dashboards, icon walls, and stock-photo hero pages.
- Red applied to several unrelated elements on one page.
- Decorative grid lines that do not align titles, evidence, or annotations.
- Rasterized text, charts, tables, or decision structures that PPTD can represent natively.
- External template pages, font files, images, logos, or brand assets copied into the pack.

## Composition finish

Treat an analytical slide as a small argument: a claim at the top, a dominant exhibit, and a concise readout or next decision. A second exhibit must add evidence (a reconciled breakdown, a comparison table, an exception or a test), not repeat the same headline. Do not fill every page with equally sized cards. Keep a quiet synthesis or section break between dense runs.

Use a shared baseline for chart labels, aligned numeric columns and a limited set of type sizes. Distinguish a section label, headline, exhibit label, evidence text and source note by size and spacing before adding color. Keep body text readable; short exhibit labels may be smaller. Avoid single-word last lines, detached punctuation and arbitrary line breaks. Reflow Chinese and English separately.

Put units, population and measurement window beside the relevant exhibit. Directly label the meaningful exception or inflection; use a subtle highlight or a short leader without crossing another label. For paired exhibits with different denominators, state both explicitly. Reconcile subtotals and deltas. Use a common quantitative scale; decorative bars must never imply a different value.

End an evidence page with a concise interpretation, bounded recommendation or open question. A roadmap names owners and a gate; a scorecard distinguishes exceptions from measures that pass. Highlight the one decision or exception, not every cell. Adapt these devices to the parent pack: quiet serif pages, engineering schematics and bold posters should not converge on one visual style.

Inspect a rendered page, not just its bounding boxes: check the reading order, line endings, label collisions, chart contrast and the gap above the footer. Keep labels clear of rules and markers. Use native editable text and geometry. Fictional reference data must be replaced by verified user material or visibly marked as an example.
